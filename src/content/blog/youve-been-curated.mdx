---
title: You've been curated
date: 2025-09-07
description: 'The next era of recommendations'
---

After interviewing twenty-five consumer founders, a pattern emerged: everyone is thinking about recommendations differently now.

TikTok changed the game. Comprehensive user profiling drives discovery with unprecedented precision. The algorithm knows what you want before you know you want it. Engagement metrics refined to the millisecond.

Yet TikTok's dominance creates opportunities for alternatives. Not everyone wants to be profiled. Not every decision benefits from algorithmic optimization. Sometimes you want recommendations from sources you trust rather than systems you feed.

Three strategies are emerging:

Social recommendations leverage trust gradients. Close friends carry different weight than acquaintances who carry different weight than strangers with shared interests. Belli and Airbuds explore this space—what do people you actually know actually like?

Community recommendations organize around identity and interest. Fizz for college students. Strava for athletes. The community context shapes what gets surfaced. Your running club's favorite shoes matter more than what the algorithm thinks runners generally buy.

Authority recommendations elevate human curation. Experts, tastemakers, critics—people who've earned trust through demonstrated judgment. Match their taste profile to yours, and their recommendations transfer.

The counterintuitive insight: as algorithmic personalization intensifies, human curation becomes more valuable, not less. The algorithm gives you more of what you've already engaged with. Human curators can give you something you'd never find on your own.

TikTok optimizes for engagement. Engagement isn't always what you want. Sometimes you want quality. Sometimes you want surprise. Sometimes you want the thing your most interesting friend discovered before anyone else knew about it.

The next generation of recommendation products won't out-compete TikTok's algorithms. They'll offer escape hatches. Contexts where social signals, community knowledge, or expert taste matter more than what keeps you scrolling.

The feed will always exist. But the best things might come from somewhere else.
