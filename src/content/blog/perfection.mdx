---
title: Perfection
date: 2026-01-12
description: 'Adhering to a higher standard of excellence'
---

Late nights at the Executive Office Building, finalizing realfood.gov and a new Food Pyramid. The work demanded something different—not speed, but precision.

The final 5% of polish transforms a functional site into one that feels trustworthy. Pixel-perfect layouts. Refined animations. Accessibility considerations that most users will never consciously notice but will feel in every interaction.

Typography received obsessive attention—the weight of headings, the rhythm of body text, the breathing room between paragraphs. Spacing that guides the eye naturally. Motion curves that feel organic rather than mechanical. Performance optimization that makes pages snap into place.

Responsive design that doesn't just adapt but genuinely considers how someone on a phone in a rural clinic needs information presented differently than someone on a desktop in an office.

This isn't about celebrating novelty. It's about earning public trust through invisible craftsmanship.

Startup culture rushes to ship. Get the MVP out. Iterate later. Move fast, break things. There's wisdom in that approach for certain contexts.

Government work demands different values. When you're building something that millions of Americans will use to make decisions about their health, their benefits, their civic participation—details signal respect for citizens' time and attention.

The polish isn't vanity. It's a promise that we took this seriously. That we thought about edge cases. That we tested with real users. That we didn't cut corners.

Excellence isn't perfection for its own sake. It's the accumulated weight of a thousand small decisions made correctly.
