title: Away
date: 2025-11-05
description: 'Reflecting on the last month away from the startup ecosystem'
---

One month into working for the National Design Studio in Washington DC. Time enough to gain perspective on what I left behind.

Silicon Valley's startup culture has become something I barely recognize from when I first entered it. Venture capital's excess created an incestuous ecosystem that prioritizes attention-grabbing over substance. The incentives reward performance over production.

Twitter threads about "learnings." LinkedIn posts celebrating funding rounds for products that don't exist yet. Conference talks about disruption from people who've never built anything that disrupted their own morning routine.

Performative innovation. The appearance of building the future while optimizing for engagement metrics.

Government work offers a strange relief. No one here is trying to go viral. The goal isn't to generate buzz—it's to build software that works for people who need it to work. Veterans checking their benefits. Seniors navigating Medicare. Citizens registering to vote.

The frustrations are different. Bureaucracy is real. Procurement is a nightmare. Legacy systems demand respect even when they deserve none. Progress happens slowly, measured in quarters rather than sprints.

But the satisfaction is different too. When we ship something, it serves Americans at scale. Not a niche of early adopters or a demographic slice attractive to advertisers. Actual people with actual needs.

This isn't Twitter-worthy work. No one will write a breathless profile about government form optimization. The dopamine hits of startup life—the launches, the press, the funding announcements—don't exist here.

What exists instead is the quiet knowledge that the work matters. That's enough.

We're hiring, by the way. Engineering, design, project management, creative roles. If building things that matter appeals more than building things that trend, reach out.
