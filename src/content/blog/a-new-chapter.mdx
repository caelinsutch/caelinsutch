I'm joining the National Design Studio as an engineer.

The decision came after months of deliberation. Should I start another company? The itch was there—ideas accumulated, potential co-founders circled, the familiar pull of building something from nothing.

But I kept returning to a different question: what do I actually want from work right now?

I identified my requirements. Autonomy to make decisions without endless approval chains. A small team where individual contributions visibly matter. Product focus rather than process theater. Agency to shape what we build and how we build it.

I interviewed with fifteen-plus founders. Good people building interesting things. Three offers materialized, each compelling in different ways.

I chose NDS based on factors that sound irrational when stated plainly: lore and FOMO.

The lore: this team has built things I admire. They've shipped work that influenced how I think about design and engineering. The opportunity to learn from people operating at that level doesn't come often.

The FOMO: if this moment in government technology passes without my participation, I'll regret it. The window for transforming how government builds software is open now in ways it wasn't before and might not be again.

The team is world-class. I don't use that phrase casually. These are people who could work anywhere, choosing to work on Social Security interfaces and national park websites and voter registration systems and Medicare portals.

I'm relocating to DC. Keeping my NYC connections—the city remains home in ways that matter. But the work is in Washington, so that's where I'll be.

This feels like the beginning of something significant. Government technology at a pivotal moment. A team assembled to seize that moment. The chance to build software that serves everyone, not just the demographic slices that venture capital finds attractive.

Let's see what we can build.
