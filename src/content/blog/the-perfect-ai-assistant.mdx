---
title: The Perfect AI Assistant
date: 2025-09-17
description: 'What makes AI worth interacting with long-term'
---

The best AI assistants won't feel like chat interfaces. They'll feel like infrastructure—always present, occasionally visible, continuously useful.

I've been thinking about this through the lens of "Always On" computing. Proactive systems. Event-driven architectures. Software that operates beyond the synchronous request-response pattern of traditional chat.

Poke went viral recently, and studying it reveals both promise and problems. The product offers personality-driven onboarding—you choose an assistant persona that shapes all subsequent interactions. iMessage integration means you interact through a channel you already use constantly. Background task management handles things without requiring your active attention.

The event architecture excites me. AI that notices things, tracks things, acts on things—without waiting for you to ask.

But weaknesses emerge on examination. The differentiation from existing tools remains unclear. Why Poke over the assistants already embedded in my devices? The economics seem unsustainable—low consumer pricing against high compute costs creates a path to either raising prices or degrading quality. Most critically, it lacks habit-forming features. Novel interactions don't automatically become necessary interactions.

I've been building my own version. Poppy is an iMessage bot with modular architecture supporting multiple channels and multi-user functionality. The initial focus is restaurant reservations—finding availability, booking autonomously, managing the friction of getting a table at places that are otherwise impossible.

Reservations are a wedge. The broader vision is an assistant that shares context across all my devices and interaction modalities. Text it. Talk to it. Let it watch my calendar and email and location. Have it surface information before I know I need it.

The perfect AI assistant doesn't wait to be asked. It understands enough about your life to anticipate what you need and handles it invisibly. Chat is a fallback for edge cases, not the primary interface.

We're not there yet. But the architecture is becoming clear.
